\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item When someone changes the meaning of a word or phrase from one part of the argument to another that person may have committed which of the following fallacies?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Hypostatization
    \item Appeal to authority
    \item Red herring fallacy
    \item False cause fallacy
    \item Straw man fallacy
\end{enumerate}
\item Singer claims that his argument upsets the traditional distinction between:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item fairness and bias.
    \item ethical and unethical.
    \item duty and charity.
    \item positive and negative rights.
    \item freedom and restriction.
\end{enumerate}
\item The universe, like a watch, must have a maker.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Fallacy of Accident
    \item Questionable Analogy
    \item Questionable Cause
    \item Fallacy of Composition
\end{enumerate}
\item In Butler's view, acting virtuously consists of acting in accordance with:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item all of our inward principles.
    \item our highest inward principle.
    \item the principles dictated by law.
    \item the commands of God.
    \item the principles of our elders.
\end{enumerate}
\item In Singer's view, the question of when it's wrong to painlessly kill an animal is one
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item that is irrelevant as it is never wrong to painlessly kill an animal.
    \item that is only wrong if the animal is a pet.
    \item that is entirely dependent on the species of the animal.
    \item that is dependent on the cultural norms of a society.
    \item that is ethically unimportant.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
