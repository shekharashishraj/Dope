\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item When a claim is supported only by asserting that unidentified experts accept the claim the
arguer has committed which of the following fallacies?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item snob appeal
    \item appeal to popularity
    \item post hoc ergo propter hoc
    \item appeal to anonymous authority
    \item straw man
\end{enumerate}
\item When someone responds to your argument with a sarcastic statement such as, "Yeah, right.
Like that's ever going to happen," that person may have committed which fallacy?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item tu quoque
    \item appeal to ignorance
    \item argumentum ad populum
    \item false cause
    \item appeal to indignation
\end{enumerate}
\item Cicero argues that we will never go morally astray if we:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item follow religious edicts.
    \item follow historical precedents.
    \item follow the teachings of philosophers.
    \item follow Nature as our guide.
    \item follow our conscience.
\end{enumerate}
\item In a faulty analogy, the things being compared are...
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Completely unrelated in any way
    \item Similar in some ways, different in others
    \item Similar in appearance, but not in function
    \item Sufficiently similar
    \item Not sufficiently similar in relevant ways
\end{enumerate}
\item According to Lukianoff and Haidt, the recent trend to uncover microaggressions encourages
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item students to confabulate reasons.
    \item the pursuit of justice by marking out racism, sexism, and classism.
    \item labeling, by assigning global negative traits to persons.
    \item universities to bear overly burdensome legal obligations.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
