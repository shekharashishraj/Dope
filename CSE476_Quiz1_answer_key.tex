\item \textbf{Answer:} A
\\ \textit{Options:} A) Beginning of an entity; B) Inside an entity; C) Outside any entity; D) Between two tokens
\\ \textit{Note:} Correct option: A - Beginning of an entity. In BIO tagging, B- marks the first token of an entity, I- marks tokens inside an entity, and O marks tokens outside any entity.
\item \textbf{Answer:} C
\\ \textit{Options:} A) It uses only words (observations) and ignores labels; B) It is identical to an HMM; C) It models transitions between labels but does not consider the words; D) It models emissions P(word | label)
\\ \textit{Note:} Correct option: C - It models transitions between labels but does not consider the words. A plain Markov chain only models state (label) transitions, unlike an HMM which also includes emission probabilities.
\item \textbf{Answer:} D
\\ \textit{Options:} A) P(label | previous label); B) P(label | word); C) P(word | previous word); D) P(word | label)
\\ \textit{Note:} Correct option: D - P(word | label). Emission probability in an HMM is the probability of observing a word given a hidden state (label). Option A describes transition probability.
\item \textbf{Answer:} A
\\ \textit{Options:} A) A task that identifies predicates in a sentence and labels roles of elements such as agent, theme, or location; B) A process that assigns grammatical roles like subject and object based on syntactic structure; C) A method that classifies named entities and links them to a knowledge base; D) A technique that labels words with their parts of speech and morphological features
\\ \textit{Note:} Correct option: A - Semantic Role Labeling (SRL) identifies predicates (verbs) and labels the semantic roles of their arguments, such as agent (who does the action), theme (what is affected), location (where), etc.
\item \textbf{Answer:} B
\\ \textit{Options:} A) A technique that compresses text by replacing consecutive repeated characters with a count and a symbol; B) A tokenization method that iteratively merges the most frequent pair of symbols in a corpus; C) A tokenization method that splits text based on whitespace and punctuation; D) A character-level encoding scheme that assigns fixed-length binary codes to characters
\\ \textit{Note:} Correct option: B - Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) is a tokenization algorithm that starts with character-level tokens and iteratively merges the most frequent pair of adjacent symbols, creating subword units that balance vocabulary size and sequence length.
\item \textbf{Answer:} B
\\ \textit{Options:} A) To label each token with a grammatical category; B) To convert text into a sequence of symbols suitable for modeling; C) To reduce vocabulary size to zero; D) To assign probabilities to sentences
\\ \textit{Note:} Correct option: B - Tokenization converts raw text into discrete symbols (tokens) that can be processed by NLP models.

\item \textbf{Answer:} D
\\ \textit{Options:} A) Part-of-speech tagging; B) Named Entity Recognition; C) Dependency parsing; D) Sentiment analysis
\\ \textit{Note:} Correct option: D - Sentiment analysis is a classification task, not a sequence tagging task. Dependency parsing, while related to sequences, is typically considered a parsing task rather than sequence tagging.

\\ \textit{Note:} Correct option: A - BIO tagging assigns exactly one label per token, making it impossible to represent nested or overlapping entities (e.g., "New York University" containing both "New York" and the full name).
