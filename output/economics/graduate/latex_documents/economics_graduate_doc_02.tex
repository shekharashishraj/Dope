\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item With respect to the kinked demand curve, which of the following is NOT correct?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item There is no price leader.
    \item There are relatively equal shares of the market.
    \item Price is greater than marginal costs.
    \item The demand curve is of one continuous function.
\end{enumerate}
\item Is the achievement of full employment sufficient for the maintenance of growth?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Growth only depends on full employment
    \item Yes, full employment guarantees growth
    \item Full employment always leads to increasing per capita output
    \item No, full employment is not sufficient for the maintenance of growth
\end{enumerate}
\item Which of the following is true about a price floor?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item It will be located in the middle of the equilibrium price.
    \item It will be located below the equilibrium price.
    \item It will be located above the equilibrium price.
    \item It will be located above the market price.
    \item It is used when the equilibrium price is too high.
\end{enumerate}
\item When the price of pears increases, we expect the following:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Quantity supplied of pears falls.
    \item The price of apples falls.
    \item There is no change in the quantity demanded or supplied.
    \item The price of pears falls.
    \item Quantity demanded of pears falls.
\end{enumerate}
\item Which of the following represents a measure of a firm's monopoly pricing or market power,
i.e., the larger the value of the index, the greater the firm's market pricing power?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item (P-MC)/MC\^{}2
    \item (P-MC\^{}2)/P
    \item (MC-P)/MC
    \item (MC-P)/MC\^{}2
    \item (MC-P)/P\^{}2
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
