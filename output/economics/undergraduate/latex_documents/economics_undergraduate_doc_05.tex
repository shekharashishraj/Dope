\section*{Multiple Choice (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item A small business estimates price elasticity of demand for the product to be 3. To raise
total revenue, owners should
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item keep the price constant as demand is elastic.
    \item decrease price as demand is inelastic.
    \item decrease the quality of the product.
    \item increase price as demand is elastic.
    \item increase price as demand is inelastic.
\end{enumerate}
\item Suppose the government seeks to achieve a balanced budgetby levying taxes of $100 billion
and making expendituresof $100 billion. How will this affect NNP if MPC = 0.8?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Decrease of \$500 billion in NNP
    \item Increase of \$500 billion in NNP
    \item Decrease of \$100 billion in NNP
    \item No change in NNP because taxes cancel out expenditures
    \item Increase of \$200 billion in NNP
\end{enumerate}
\item A minimum wage in the market for fast-food workers is likely to produce
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item a decrease in the demand for fast-food workers.
    \item a stabilization in the market for fast-food workers.
    \item a decrease in the supply of fast-food workers.
    \item a decrease in the prices of fast-food items.
    \item no change in the market for fast-food workers.
\end{enumerate}
\item Which one of the following is a disadvantage of the general to specific or "LSE"
("Hendry") approach to building econometric models, relative to the specific to general
approach?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item Some variables may be excluded at the first stage leading to coefficient biases
    \item The final model may lack theoretical interpretation
    \item The final model may be statistically inadequate
    \item If the initial model is mis-specified, all subsequent steps will be invalid.
\end{enumerate}
\item If 1964 GNP = $622 billion and the average money supply was$155 billion, what was the
income velocity of money?
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item 4.5 times
    \item 3 times
    \item 4.0129 times
    \item 7 times
    \item 2 times
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
