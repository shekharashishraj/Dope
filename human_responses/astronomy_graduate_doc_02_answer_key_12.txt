Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

Eratosthenes famously estimated the Earth's circumference around 240 B.C. by comparing the angles of the Sun's rays in two different locations in Egypt—Syene and Alexandria. 

His method involved measuring the shadow cast by a stick at local noon on the summer solstice in Alexandria, while knowing that at the same time in Syene, the Sun was directly overhead. 

By calculating the angle of the shadow and knowing the distance between the two locations, Eratosthenes used simple geometry to estimate the Earth's circumference. 

While this methodology is not directly related to mapping stars, his work laid vital groundwork for astronomical measurements by emphasizing the importance of geometric and latitude-based observations.


12. Sample answer:

The U.S.S.R.'s Mars lander missions, particularly the Mars series of probes, were crucial in the early exploration of Mars. 

The Mars 3 mission in 1971 achieved the first successful soft landing on Mars, although it transmitted data for only a few seconds. 

These missions contributed to our understanding of Mars' harsh surface conditions and the challenges of landing on the planet. 

They laid the groundwork for future missions by testing engineering solutions and advancing the technology needed for planetary exploration. 

Despite limited success in data collection, these missions provided valuable lessons that influenced subsequent Mars exploration efforts, including those by NASA.
