Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

To calculate the weight of H₂SO₄, multiply the volume of the solution by its density and H₂SO₄ percentage: 

Weight of H₂SO₄ = 500 mL × 1.8 g/mL × 0.90 = 810 g.



Molarity: Calculate by dividing mass by molar mass of H₂SO₄ (98.079 g/mol), then divide by volume in liters. 

Molarity = 810 g / 98.079 g/mol / 0.5 L ≈ 16.5 M.

Normality: For sulfuric acid, normality is double the molarity (as it can donate 2 H⁺ ions), which leads to a normality of approximately 33 N.


12. Sample answer:

Use the formula φ = hc/λ, where h is Planck's constant and c is the speed of light. 



Work function φ = (6.63 × 10^{-34} J·s × 3 × 10^8 m/s) / (564 × 10^{-9} m) ≈ 3.52 × 10^{-19} J.



Implications: Determines the minimum energy needed for photoelectric emission from sodium, critical for understanding electron emissions in the photoelectric effect.
