Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

A black body in astronomy is an idealized object that absorbs all electromagnetic radiation, regardless of frequency or angle of incidence. 

It emits radiation at a characteristic spectrum that depends only on its temperature, described by Planck's law. 

In astronomy, stars are often treated as black bodies to model their radiation, as this approximation helps in determining the temperature and energy output of stars by analyzing their spectrum. 

Understanding the black body radiation of stars allows astronomers to infer various stellar properties like temperature, size, and luminosity, contributing to our knowledge of stellar life cycles and the dynamics of star systems.


12. Sample answer:

Venus has a very slow rotational speed, taking about 243 Earth days to complete one rotation on its axis, which is longer than its orbital period around the Sun. 

Despite this slow rotation, Venus's surface temperature remains uniformly high, averaging around 467°C (872°F) due to its thick atmosphere rich in carbon dioxide, creating a strong greenhouse effect. 

The slow rotation means that there is minimal temperature variation between day and night across the planet's surface. 

Instead, the high surface temperature is primarily a result of its dense atmosphere enabling efficient heat distribution, influenced by strong atmospheric winds. 

This greenhouse effect and associated geophysical characteristics of Venus impact its geological activity and atmospheric dynamics, making it one of the hottest planets in the solar system.
