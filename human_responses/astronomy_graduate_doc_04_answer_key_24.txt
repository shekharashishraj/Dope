Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

A white dwarf is the final evolutionary state of stars whose mass is not high enough to become neutron stars or black holes. 

They are the remnants of low and medium mass stars, typically about the size of Earth in volume but with a mass comparable to that of the Sun. 

White dwarfs are composed mostly of electron-degenerate matter and no longer undergo fusion reactions. 

They radiate energy that is initially derived from residual thermal energy, gradually cooling and fading over time. 

White dwarfs are significant because they help astronomers understand the lifecycle of stars, the processes of nuclear fusion, and the nature of degeneracy pressure in stellar remnants. 

Studying their radiation and thermal properties provides insights into the future of our own Sun.


12. Sample answer:

Mars's atmosphere is thin, with a pressure of about 0.6% that of Earth's atmospheric pressure at sea level. 

This low atmospheric pressure is partly due to Mars's lack of a significant global magnetic field. 

A strong magnetic field can protect a planet's atmosphere from being eroded by solar wind. 

Mars's weak and localized magnetic fields, present only in some regions of the crust, fail to provide such protection. 

This loss of atmospheric pressure affects Mars's ability to retain heat, influencing its surface temperature and climate. 

Additionally, the weak magnetic field impacts the geophysical surface processes, including erosion and sediment transportation, and affects the planet’s capacity to support liquid water. 

Understanding these elements is crucial for assessing Mars's habitability and geological history.
