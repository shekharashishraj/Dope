Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

A black body in astronomy refers to an idealized object that absorbs all incident electromagnetic radiation, regardless of frequency or angle of incidence, and re-radiates this energy perfectly. 

Black bodies are perfect emitters of radiation, characterized by a continuous spectrum. 

The radiation emitted by a black body is described by Planck's law, and the distribution depends solely on the temperature of the body. 

In astronomy, understanding black body radiation helps scientists determine the temperature, composition, and energy output of stars. 

By examining the spectrum of a star, astronomers can approximate its surface temperature and other properties, leading to insights about its structure and life cycle. 

The Sun, for example, is often modeled as a black body with an approximate temperature of 5778 K.


12. Sample answer:

Venus has an extremely slow rotational speed, taking about 243 Earth days to complete one rotation, which is longer than its orbital period around the Sun. 

This slow rotation has significant implications for its atmospheric dynamics and geophysical characteristics. 

Venus's atmosphere is incredibly dense and consists mainly of carbon dioxide, leading to a surface pressure about 92 times that of Earth's. 

The dense atmosphere, combined with the slow rotation, contributes to a phenomenon known as super-rotation, where the atmosphere circulates the planet much faster than the planet itself rotates. 

This affects the heat distribution across the planet, contributing to uniform surface temperatures despite the axial rotation. 

The high pressure and slow rotation also influence geological activity, impacting surface erosion, formation of surface features, and potentially inhibiting plate tectonics. 

Overall, Venus's rotation and atmospheric dynamics create a challenging environment for exploration and offer insights into planetary evolution and climate.
