11. Sample answer:

**  
Discuss the methodology employed by Eratosthenes to estimate the Earth's size in 240 B.C., including the significance of his observations in different latitudes.  


- Eratosthenes calculated the Earth's circumference by comparing the angle of the Sun's rays at noon in two different locations: Syene (now Aswan) and Alexandria, Egypt. 

At noon in Syene, the Sun was directly overhead, casting no shadow, while in Alexandria, the Sun cast a shadow corresponding to an angle of approximately 7.2 degrees. 

Knowing the distance between Syene and Alexandria, he used simple geometry to estimate the Earth's circumference, deducing that 7.2 degrees is 1/50th of a circle. 

Thus, he multiplied the distance by 50 to obtain the Earth's total circumference.

12. Sample answer:

**  
Discuss the historical significance and scientific contributions of the U.S.S.R.'s lander missions to Venus, analyzing their impact on our understanding of the planet.  


- The U.S.S.R.'s Venera program significantly advanced our understanding of Venus. 

The Venera landers were the first to transmit data from the surface of another planet, providing crucial information on the temperature, pressure, and atmospheric composition of Venus. 

These missions revealed the extreme conditions on Venus's surface, such as high temperatures and pressures, which were much more severe than previously thought. 

This contributed to our understanding of Venus’s greenhouse effect and atmospheric characteristics, which provided valuable comparisons for Earth’s atmospheric and climate studies. 

The Venera missions marked a milestone in planetary exploration, setting a precedent for future interplanetary missions.
