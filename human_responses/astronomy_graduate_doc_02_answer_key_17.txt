Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

Ptolemy did not actually estimate the Earth's size in 240 B.C.—it was Eratosthenes who did. 

Eratosthenes measured the Earth's circumference by comparing the angle of the Sun at noon in two different locations, Alexandria and Syene. 

He observed that in Syene, the Sun was directly overhead, while in Alexandria, it cast a shadow. 

By measuring the angle of the shadow and knowing the distance between the two cities, he calculated the Earth's circumference. 

This was a significant advancement, as it employed a geometric method to derive the Earth's size with remarkable accuracy for the time. 

It illustrated the concept of a spherical Earth and helped pave the way for future exploration and navigation.


12. Sample answer:

The USA's lander missions to Venus, such as the Pioneer Venus project, conducted extensive studies of Venus's atmosphere and surface. 

These missions were significant because they provided unprecedented data about Venus's extreme surface conditions, characterized by high temperatures and pressures, and an atmosphere composed mainly of carbon dioxide with clouds of sulfuric acid. 

The information gathered helped scientists understand the greenhouse effect in Venus's atmosphere and drew parallels to environmental conditions on Earth. 

Moreover, these missions laid the groundwork for future exploration and increased the understanding of planetary atmospheres, contributing to comparative planetology.
