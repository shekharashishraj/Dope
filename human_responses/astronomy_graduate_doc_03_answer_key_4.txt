11. Sample answer:

The Laniakea Supercluster is a massive cosmic structure that encompasses the Milky Way and thousands of other galaxies. 

It demonstrates how galaxies and galaxy clusters are not randomly distributed but rather form part of larger interconnected networks. 

This has implications for our understanding of cosmic structure, indicating there are gravitational forces at play on such scales, and suggesting a more cohesive view of the universe's architecture. 

It provides insights into the distribution of matter in the universe and challenges scientists to understand the dynamics governing large-scale structures.


12. Sample answer:

Pluto’s long orbital period means that its seasons are prolonged, with each lasting over 60 Earth years. 

This extended seasonality affects atmospheric escape and redistribution processes. 

During its orbit, variations in solar heating can cause extensive sublimation of surface ices, altering its atmosphere over time. 

Changes in the distance from the Sun also influence temperature and atmospheric pressure, which could significantly affect geological processes. 

Understanding these dynamics is crucial for comprehending how lengthy orbital periods impact other distant celestial bodies similarly.
