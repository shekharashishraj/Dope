Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

A white dwarf is the remnant core of a star that has exhausted its nuclear fuel and shed its outer layers. 

Typically, white dwarfs are the endpoints of stellar evolution for stars with masses up to about 8 solar masses. 

They are composed mostly of electron-degenerate matter, which allows them to resist further collapse. 

Despite their small size, white dwarfs are quite hot, initially glowing brightly due to residual thermal energy. 

Over time, they cool and fade. Understanding white dwarfs is crucial for astrophysics as they help explain the lifecycle of stars, the end stages of stellar evolution, and the dynamics of binary star systems.


12. Sample answer:

Mars’s current rotational period is approximately 24.6 hours, similar to Earth's, but its magnetic field is weak compared to Earth's. 

The relationship between rotational speed and magnetic field strength in terrestrial planets is often related to the dynamo effect, which requires a molten, convecting metallic core and rapid rotation. 

Mars may have once had a strong magnetic field that diminished as its core cooled and solidified, ceasing convection. 

This lack of a strong magnetic field increases Mars's exposure to solar and cosmic radiation, affecting its atmosphere and surface conditions, contributing to the loss of atmospheric particles to space.
