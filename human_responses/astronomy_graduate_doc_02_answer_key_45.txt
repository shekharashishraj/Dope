Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

Ptolemy did not actually estimate the Earth's size in 240 B.C.; this is commonly attributed to Eratosthenes. 

Eratosthenes used the difference in the angle of the sun's rays at two locations (Alexandria and Syene) at noon on the summer solstice. 

By measuring the angle of the shadow in Alexandria and knowing that there was no shadow in Syene, he calculated the Earth's circumference. 

The significance lay in his understanding that the Earth was spherical and could be measured using geometric principles.


12. Sample answer:

The USA’s lander missions, particularly the Magellan mission, provided detailed radar maps of Venus, highlighting its volcanic and tectonic activity. 

These missions significantly advanced our understanding of Venus's atmosphere, surface conditions, and geology, revealing a planet with a harsh climate and extreme surface temperatures. 

They helped scientists compare Venus’s geological features with Earth’s, offering insights into planetary evolution and the greenhouse effect.
