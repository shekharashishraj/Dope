Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

The Zagmuk festival in ancient Babylonian culture was significant for several reasons. 

Religiously, it was a time to honor the chief god Marduk, who was believed to battle chaos for the coming year. 

Socially, it reinforced the power of the king, who performed rituals to ensure Marduk's victory. 

Culturally, it was a major celebration that marked the New Year, involving public gatherings, feasts, and ceremonies that strengthened community bonds.


12. Sample answer:

The Enuma Elish, a Babylonian creation epic, portrays Marduk as both wrathful and merciful. 

Marduk's wrath is evident in his battle against Tiamat, where he uses his power to defeat the forces of chaos. 

This demonstrates his role as a fierce protector. On the other hand, his mercy is shown through the creation of the world and humanity, where he establishes order and provides for mankind. 

Marduk's dual nature highlights his complexity as a deity who upholds justice while ensuring benevolence.
