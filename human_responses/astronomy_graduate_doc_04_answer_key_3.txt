Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

A white dwarf is a stellar remnant composed mostly of electron-degenerate matter. 

It is the final evolutionary state for stars not massive enough to become neutron stars or black holes. 

White dwarfs hold significance in understanding the lifecycle of stars and their radiation, as they provide insights into the thermal properties of degenerate matter. 

Over time, they cool and fade away, illustrating the final stages of stellar evolution.


12. Sample answer:

Mars has a thin atmosphere and a weak magnetic field due to its smaller size and loss of internal heat. 

The weak magnetic field contributes to the atmospheric loss as it fails to provide adequate protection from solar winds, which strip away the atmosphere over time. 

Consequently, the low atmospheric pressure influences the planet's surface conditions and limits the possibility of liquid water. 

Understanding these factors helps explain the planet's geophysical and climatic characteristics.
