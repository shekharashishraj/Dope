Multiple Choice Questions

1. (D) ** Which is not an essential requirement for life as we know it?
- **Answer: D: A and B** (While oxygen and sunlight are crucial for many life forms on Earth, they are not universally required for all life; some organisms can live without oxygen and derive energy from other chemical processes).

**

2. (D) ** Why do we look for water-ice in craters at Mercury's pole?
- **Answer: D: These craters contain the only permanently shadowed regions on Mercury** (These regions do not receive sunlight and may trap water-ice).

**

3. (A) ** Which of these has NOT been one of the main hypotheses considered for the origin of the Moon?
- **Answer: A: The Moon split from the Earth due to tidal forces.** (The main hypotheses include the Giant Impact Hypothesis, capture, and co-accretion, but splitting due to tidal forces is not among the primary scientific hypotheses).

**

4. (D) ** How far is Altair from the Earth?
- **Answer: D: 1.5 x 10^17 meters** (The average distance from the Earth to the Sun is about 1.5 x 10^11 meters, and multiplying by one million gives this distance to Altair).

**

5. (C) ** When was the telescope invented by Galileo?
- **Answer: C: 1609** (Galileo made significant improvements and systematic astronomical observations with the telescope in 1609).

- **Answer:** This statement is incorrect. The ecliptic is the apparent path of the Sun on the celestial sphere as seen from Earth, not the axial tilt, though the axial tilt does affect seasons.

Long Form Response Questions

11. Sample answer:

** Discuss the methodology employed by Eratosthenes to estimate the Earth's size in 240 B.C.


- **Answer:** Eratosthenes estimated the Earth's circumference by measuring the angle of the Sun's rays at two different locations (Syene and Alexandria) at the same time. 

By knowing the distance between these two cities and the angle of the shadow, he applied geometry to derive the Earth's circumference.

12. Sample answer:

** Discuss the historical significance and scientific contributions of the U.S.S.R.'s lander missions to Venus.


- **Answer:** The Soviet Union's Venera missions to Venus provided invaluable data on the planet's atmosphere, surface conditions, and heat. 

These missions revealed the harsh conditions on Venus and contributed significantly to our understanding of its geology and atmosphere, highlighting its extreme greenhouse effect.
