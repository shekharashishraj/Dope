11. Sample answer:

The rate constant \( k \) is calculated using the formula for first-order reactions: \[ k = \frac{0.693}{t_{1/2}} \] \[ k = \frac{0.693}{34 \, \text{minutes}} \approx 0.0204 \, \text{min}^{-1} \] Relationship: For first-order reactions, the half-life is constant and independent of the initial concentration. 

The rate constant is inversely proportional to the half-life.

12. Sample answer:

Principle: According to Charles's Law, the volume of a gas is directly proportional to its temperature when pressure is held constant. 

Calculation: Initial volume (\( V_1 \)) = 100 ml at 20°C (293 K) Final temperature (\( T_2 \)) = 10°C (283 K) \[ \frac{V_2}{100 \, \text{ml}} = \frac{283 \, \text{K}}{293 \, \text{K}} \] \[ V_2 \approx 96.6 \, \text{ml} \] As temperature decreases, the volume of gas decreases, demonstrating the direct relationship between volume and temperature at constant pressure.
